# Capture and organize are separate jobs

> [!abstract] The claim
> Deciding *where a thought goes* at the moment you have it destroys the thought.

## Why it holds

Capture and organization run on different mental gears. Capture is fast,
associative, and cheap. Organization is slow, evaluative, and expensive. Forcing
them into the same moment means the expensive one throttles the cheap one — you
stop writing things down because filing them is annoying, and a second brain
that isn't fed is just a folder.

Separating them also improves the filing. A week of distance is a free relevance
filter: most captures turn out not to matter, and you can see that clearly on
Sunday in a way you cannot on Tuesday.

## Where it breaks

Deferral only works if the deferred pile actually gets processed. An inbox you
never empty is worse than filing as you go, because now you have the illusion of
a system plus the anxiety of a backlog. The habit that makes this note true is
the [[The weekly review is the only load-bearing habit|weekly review]] — without
it, this is just permission to make a mess.

It also breaks for genuinely time-critical items. A meeting time doesn't belong
in an inbox; it belongs in a calendar. Capture is for *thinking*, not logistics.
