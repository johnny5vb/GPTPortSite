# Links carry meaning that folders cannot

> [!abstract] The claim
> A folder can express exactly one relationship — *contains* — and forces every
> note to pick a single parent. Links express any relationship and cost nothing.

## Why it holds

Ideas belong to many contexts at once. A note about pacing in a brand film is
simultaneously about editing, about attention, and about a specific client. A
folder tree makes you pick one and lose the other two. Links let the note sit in
all three neighbourhoods, and let you say *how* it relates — "contradicts",
"is a special case of", "was the reason we chose X".

The practical consequence: use folders for **lifecycle state** (inbox → notes →
archive), because a note really does have exactly one lifecycle state at a time.
Use links for **meaning**, because meaning is many-to-many.

Maps of Content are the missing piece. A MOC is just a note that curates links
to other notes — a folder you can editorialize, annotate, and put a note into
from ten different angles. See [[Second Brain MOC]] for a working example.

## Where it breaks

A link network with no entry points is unnavigable — you can only find things you
already remember. Purely link-based vaults hit this wall around a few hundred
notes, and the fix is exactly the thing the method was reacting against: some
top-down structure, in the form of MOCs.

Links also rot silently. A note whose links all point to abandoned seeds looks
connected and isn't. The monthly MOC prune exists for this.
